Cab Regret: zero cars, many regrets

A review-and-comparison blog about getting around San Francisco without a car. I took the rides, kept the receipts, and designed and coded the whole site, from sketches and Figma to a live page.

The brief

Build a review blog that compares three things on three shared points, for a specific audience. The assignment started on paper: three topic ideas, each with three things to review, three points of comparison, and a target audience.

My idea

I'm a USF student without a car, so every trip in San Francisco turns into math. Walk, wait for a bus, or pay someone to drive me a few miles? I wanted to answer that with my own receipts instead of guesses.

What I settled on

  • Three things reviewed: Uber / Lyft, Muni (the 38R), and Waymo.
  • Three points of comparison: cost, time, and effort.
  • Audience: students without a car.
  • Voice: funny and brutally honest, with no pretending a number is better than it is.

Process

From a paper sketch to a design system

1. Sketch the structure

I drew the homepage and the review page by hand before opening Figma. The ideas that survived to the final site are all on the page: tab navigation, a blackletter masthead, a running banner under it, and a Cost / Time / Effort row on every review.

My first note on the sketch says "black and white newspaper style." That changed once I started on the moodboard.

2. Moodboard

I collected inspiration sites for layout, tested four display typefaces for the logo, and set up a color palette with a job for each color.

4. Wireframes to high fidelity

I wireframed the homepage and the review page in Figma first, then added color, type and real copy in the high-fidelity version. I designed the review page as a reusable template, so every ride follows the same structure.

Sketch. Homepage on paper: tab navigation, a blackletter masthead, a running banner under it, and an intro beside cover images.

Build

Coded with only the CSS we'd learned in class

The site is plain HTML and CSS on GitHub Pages, in four pages: Home, Uber / Lyft, Muni and Waymo.

The assignment limited me to the CSS covered in class. That meant tag selectors only, with no class names, no grid, no custom properties and no pseudo-elements.

So the heading tags did double duty. I styled h5 as the blog name, h6 as the dark banner bar and h4 as the teal quote box. All five colors and all three fonts come from the system above.

From mockup to code

What changed on the way

  • Real photos replaced placeholders. The mockups had gray photo boxes. The live site uses my own photos and my own ride receipts.
  • The layout got simpler. In Figma the three entry cards sat side by side and the review page had a sidebar. In code they stack in one column, and the stat boxes sit beside the receipt screenshot.
  • The moodboard's flat two-color imagery idea didn't make it. The receipts and photos carry the visual story instead.
  • The type settled. The moodboard tested four display faces and used CC Timeline as a reference. The final site uses three Google Fonts.
